## AI

[9] OpenAI ships the GPT-5.6 family: Sol, Terra and Luna

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 in three tiers — Sol as the flagship, Terra as a cheaper capable model, and Luna as the fast, low-cost option — claiming state-of-the-art results in coding, knowledge work and science while burning fewer tokens. The pitch is performance-per-dollar rather than raw capability alone, which is the metric that actually moves product margins. For PMs, the immediate question is whether Terra or Luna now covers workloads you are currently paying flagship prices for.

SRC: https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/

[8] Anthropic restores Claude Fable 5 after US lifts jailbreak-linked export controls

Access to Claude Fable 5 (and Mythos 5) was restored on 1 July after the export controls tied to jailbreak concerns were lifted, ending a period where the top-tier model was unavailable to some customers. It is a reminder that model availability is now a policy variable, not just a vendor roadmap item. Anyone building on a single frontier model should have a documented fallback.

SRC: https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5

[8] OpenAI's GPT-Live brings full-duplex voice to the stack

GPT-Live shipped alongside the GPT-5.6 family: a voice model on a full-duplex architecture that listens, speaks and reasons simultaneously, with real-time translation, live web search mid-conversation, and the ability to hand tasks to other agents. Full-duplex removes the turn-taking latency that has made voice agents feel unusable in support and sales flows. Worth prototyping if you have any voice surface on the roadmap.

SRC: https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-july-13-2026

[7] Z.ai's GLM-5.2 reignites the "is China catching up" debate

GLM-5.2 is being held up as evidence that Chinese labs are closing the gap, delivering capability comparable to Western frontier models at a fraction of the price. The debate matters commercially more than geopolitically for most teams: a cheap, competent open-weight-adjacent option changes the build-vs-buy maths on inference-heavy features.

SRC: https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-july-13-2026

[7] EU sets out AI cybersecurity action plan and bans AI-generated intimate imagery

The Commission's July action plan on Cybersecurity and AI coordinates how member states and businesses handle risks from the most advanced models, and includes a call to build EU model-evaluation capacity ahead of market entry, targeted to be operational by 2027. Separately, the EU added a prohibition on AI systems generating non-consensual sexual imagery or CSAM, in force from December. If you ship image or video generation into the EU, that December date is a hard deadline.

SRC: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai

[6] FTC opens comment on AI accuracy and state-law output requirements

The FTC is seeking public comment on a proposed policy statement covering the legal implications of state laws that require companies to alter AI model outputs, with comments open until 31 July. This sits at the messy intersection of state AI rules and federal consumer-protection law, and the outcome will shape how much latitude US teams have to tune or filter model behaviour by jurisdiction.
